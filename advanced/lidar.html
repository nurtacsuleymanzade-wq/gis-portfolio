LiDAR processing — denoise, classify, DTM / DSM

A real USGS 3DEP airborne LiDAR tile (Boulder, Colorado; about 380 × 380 m, 3.6 million points) processed end-to-end with PDAL.

Open 3D point-cloud viewer

Pipeline (PDAL 2.x in Docker)

  1. readers.ept: stream only the area of interest from the public USGS Entwine dataset (no full-tile download), reproject to UTM 13N + NAVD88.
  2. Original vendor classes are wiped (Classification = 0) so the result shows only my own processing.
  3. Noise removal: filters.outlier (statistical, k = 12, 2.5σ) + filters.elm (extended local minimum) flag isolated and low-outlier returns as class 7. That is 96,555 points (2.7 %), removed from the products.
  4. Ground: filters.smrf (Simple Morphological Filter: slope 0.2, window 16 m, threshold 0.45 m).
  5. Buildings / vegetation: filters.hag_nn computes height above ground. filters.approximatecoplanar tests local planarity. Points > 2.5 m that are planar → building (6), other points > 2 m → high vegetation (5), the rest → other / low (1).
  6. Rasters: writers.gdal: DSM = max of all returns, DTM = IDW of ground, both 0.5 m. DTM gaps under buildings filled with gdal_fillnodata.
Honest limits: the building/vegetation split is a rule-based heuristic. Pitched roofs and dense canopy edges are partly mixed (on this tile it under-detects some roofs). Production work adds tuned thresholds, footprints or a trained classifier, plus QA against the vendor classes.

Who benefits / value

Who benefits. Architects & engineers (bare-earth DTM and building heights for site design), mining & quarrying (stockpile/terrain surfaces), municipalities & utilities (tree canopy and building inventories, vegetation encroachment), forestry & academia (canopy height models). Value: turns raw, noisy point clouds into clean classified LAZ plus DTM/DSM rasters that drop straight into QGIS, ArcGIS or CAD.
