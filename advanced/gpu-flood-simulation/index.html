GPU 2D flood simulation — Kura–Aras confluence, Sabirabad

A 72-hour, 2D hydrodynamic flood scenario run on an NVIDIA L4 GPU over the real terrain where the Aras joins the Kura, the area that flooded badly in 2010. The output is a maximum-depth map, a time-lapse of the flood front and a photoreal Blender Cycles render.

Method

  1. Terrain: SRTM 1-arc-second (AWS Terrain Tiles / skadi), reprojected to UTM 39N at 30 m.
  2. Inflow points: where the OSM Kura and Aras centre-lines enter the model domain, snapped to the lowest DEM cell.
  3. Scenario hydrograph: 12 h rise to a peak of 2,000 m³/s (Kura) and 1,000 m³/s (Aras), 36 h plateau, 24 h recession.
  4. Solver: local-inertial shallow-water scheme (Bates et al., 2010, the LISFLOOD-FP formulation) written in CuPy, Manning n = 0.035, CFL-adaptive dt, free outflow on the boundaries.
  5. Outputs: max-depth and arrival-time GeoTIFFs, a 30-min frame stack, a web overlay and a Blender Cycles render (CUDA).

Limitations of this sample

  • Discharges are a hypothetical scenario of the same order as the 2010 flood, not observed gauge data; the model is NOT calibrated.
  • SRTM is a surface model (vegetation, embankments, and no bathymetry), so the river channel holds less water than reality and the extent is indicative only.
  • SRTM noise on this very flat lowland (about 1 m of relief per km) does not resolve the river channel, so water spreads over the floodplain near the inflow points instead of travelling down the Kura. A channel-burned DTM fixes this.
  • No levees, structures or rainfall-runoff are included. A real study would use LiDAR/DTM plus surveyed cross-sections.
