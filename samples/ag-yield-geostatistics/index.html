Spatial mixed-model analysis of a nitrogen strip trial from yield-monitor data

A worked example of the workflow for analysing harvester yield-monitor data from field trials. It uses a real public dataset: the Las Rosas corn trial (Córdoba, Argentina, 1999 and 2001), with about 3,400 GPS yield points, six nitrogen rates (N0–N5) in replicated strips, and topographic zones. Source: R package agridat, lasrosas.corn (Anselin, Bongiovanni & Lowenberg-DeBoer 2004).

Method

  1. Cleaning: outlier screen per year (beyond 3×IQR). This dataset is already pre-cleaned, so 0 points were removed. On raw harvester logs I also handle header-up/turn-row points, start/end-pass delay, GPS lag shift, overlapping swaths and moisture correction.
  2. Model: linear mixed model (statsmodels MixedLM), yield ~ N rate + topographic zone + quadratic trend surface, with replicate block as a random effect. It is fitted per season.
  3. Spatial diagnostics: Moran's I on residuals (8-nearest-neighbour weights, 999 permutations). The trend surface removes part of the autocorrelation. 2001 still shows strong structure, which is why the full delivery uses a proper spatial error model (e.g. glmmTMB/nlme with exponential or Matérn correlation, or INLA/SPDE) rather than trusting naive standard errors.
  4. Power: two-sample t-test power from the residual SD (α=0.05, power 0.8).
Limitations, stated honestly: the power figures count yield-monitor points, which are spatially autocorrelated, so the true effective sample size is smaller. For trial design, the replicate unit is the strip or plot, and plot-level power is computed from between-strip variance. Confidence intervals here come from a trend-surface model, not a full spatial covariance model, so they are probably too narrow, especially for 2001. The dataset has no as-applied, soil or imagery layers. Those joins are part of the full delivery.

What the full delivery includes

A reproducible R/Python pipeline in Git: raw yield cleaning (with a log of every removed point), as-applied map and strip-layout joins, soil, weather and NDVI covariates, spatial mixed models with random effects for field and strip and sensitivity checks, a power and replicate-number calculator for next season, and a plain-English report per batch for agronomists.
