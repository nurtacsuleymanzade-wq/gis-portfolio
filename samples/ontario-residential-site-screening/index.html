Fort Erie, Ontario: open-land constraint screening for 5–30 acre residential sites

A first-pass screening of the Town of Fort Erie, built only from OpenStreetMap (© OpenStreetMap contributors, ODbL). It shows the constraint-overlay and net-developable-area logic that a full parcel-level study would use.

Method

  1. Town boundary and land-use polygons from OSM (farmland, meadow, grass, brownfield and vacant land as candidates). Projected to NAD83 / UTM 17N.
  2. Constraints: wetlands, water and watercourses, and railways (each buffered 30 m); woodland; parks, golf, nature reserves, schools, cemeteries and industrial/military land (removed).
  3. Net area = candidate block minus the union of constraints. Kept blocks with 5–60 gross acres and 5–30 net acres.
Limitations: OSM land-use coverage in Fort Erie is incomplete, so many real candidate lands are missing and the 4 results are illustrative only. OSM blocks are not assessment parcels. Settlement-area boundaries, Official Plan designations, zoning and NPCA regulated areas are not in this sample. The 30 m buffers are placeholders, not policy setbacks.

What the full delivery includes

A packaged QGIS/ArcGIS Pro project for Simcoe County, Owen Sound and Fort Erie. It covers parcel fabric (Ontario Parcel / Teranet licence or municipal open-data parcels where available), provincial plan areas, County/Region and local OP designations and zoning, conservation-authority regulation limits (NVCA, LSRCA, Grey Sauble, NPCA), floodplain, PSW/ELC wetlands, woodlands and rail. It also includes net-developable-acre calculations, a ranked shortlist in XLSX and KMZ, PDF maps per municipality, and a dated source register noting any digitised or unavailable data.
